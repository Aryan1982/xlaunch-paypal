# Story and pacing

## The idea

Write one sentence: *"[Product] makes [audience] feel [feeling] by [core verb]."* The film is built around making the **core verb** physical. Examples of verb → visual mechanism (invent your own for each brief):

| Verb | Possible physical mechanisms |
|---|---|
| fix / correct | items lifted off the page, flagged one by one, then fixed on contact by a travelling object |
| translate / convert | one card clones into many variants during a flip; content flows from one shape into another |
| automate / process | a shape-collapse chain (input → progress → done → result); nodes popping along a connector |
| aggregate / understand | lines fanning in from many sources into one node; scattered fragments assembling into one view |
| send / share | an object launched along an arc that becomes the next scene's connector |
| generate / write | text streaming with a leading edge; words flying into place |
| find / filter | the camera pushes through a dense field to one lit element while everything else defocuses |
| organise | scattered planes snapping into a grid or stack with staggered landings |

## Beat structures

Choose a structure that fits the length and brief. These are starting points, not fixed orders.

**Problem → product → proof → logo**
Open on the friction (a crowded, overloaded or slow state), turn with the product's arrival, demonstrate the verb 1–3 times with escalating energy, land on the logo.

**One sentence across the film**
A single line of copy is split into 2–4-word beats; product moments sit *between* the words. Works for music-led teasers.

**Detail → world**
Start on a macro detail (a word, a button, a cursor) and keep pulling back to reveal the product, then the outcome.

**Single take through a space**
The camera travels along a rail through stations (input → engine → output → result), never cutting.

**Escalating passes**
Show the same set of items three times with rising energy: reveal them, flag them (fast stagger), resolve them (one per beat).

## Length templates

| Length | Shape |
|---|---|
| 10 s | hook (1.5 s) → the verb in action (4–5 s, 2–3 beats) → payoff (1.5 s) → logo hold (1–1.5 s) |
| 20 s | hook (2 s) → setup/problem (3 s) → product arrives (2 s) → verb, 2 features (7 s) → payoff (3 s) → logo (2 s) |
| 30 s | hook (2–3 s) → problem (4 s) → turn (2 s) → 3 feature beats with escalation (12 s) → summary (4 s) → logo (2.5–3 s) |

Shorter is denser: a 10 s film has no room for a problem act; show the verb immediately.

## Energy curve

Plan the energy (amount of motion on screen) per beat. Useful shapes:
- **Quiet open → building middle → fast climax → absolute stillness.** The most common benchmark shape. The opening is small and calm (small type, slow drift), the middle gets denser (typing, staggers, hops), the climax is the fastest move (a whip into the logo), and the end is a total freeze.
- **Accelerate into a release:** shorten beats toward the end (single words at 170–400 ms each), then one slow held reveal, then the logo.
- **Steady pulse with one spike:** consistent beats with one big moment (a shockwave, a clone burst) at about 60–70 % of the film.

Before the biggest hit, hold 200–330 ms of near-stillness.

## The motif

Pick one object derived from the brand or the verb: the logo's mark, a glyph, a stroke, a token, a shape, a colour of light.
1. It appears early with a function (a cursor, an icon, a highlight, a connector).
2. It travels (a comet with a trail, a hero object hopping, a line the camera rides).
3. It becomes punctuation or a container (a caret after the copy, the frame of a card).
4. It becomes or docks into the logo.

A brand mark can also be a transition system: its geometry becomes the ring, the connector lines or the wipe shapes.

## Endings

- The motif or hero object docks into the logo; the lock-up forms in motion, then settles with a long ease-out into total stillness.
- Or: a primitive (a hairline) grows into the CTA button, the label fades in, a URL types on small below.
- Or (bookend): the opening image returns, resolved.
- Hold the final card 1–3 s, fully still. No extra flourish after it has settled.

## Copy

- 2–4 words per beat, active verbs, no marketing filler ("revolutionary", "seamless").
- Use the user's own phrasing and facts from the brief and `context.md`; invent only what is needed to make UI content plausible.
- Text labels; visuals explain. If a visual already says it, cut the line.
